# 12 · Chapter Diagnostic

**A pass/fail instrument. Not a feelings checklist.**

Run this on every chapter, in this order, after the first draft and before the
line edit.

---

## 0. Rules of use

1. **Never diagnose before the draft is finished.** A chapter under construction
   will fail every gate. That's fine. That's what drafts are for.
2. **Diagnose one axis at a time.** Fixing everything at once means fixing nothing.
3. **Fail one gate → stop → fix → re-run from the top.** The gates are ordered by
   leverage.
4. **The bible diagnoses; it does not generate.** If the chapter is failing at the
   top gates, the problem is in the *story*, not the prose. Go fix the story.

---

## Gate 1 — STAKES

*Does anything cost anything in this chapter?*

- [ ] At least one character makes a **decision** (not a reaction, not a
      observation) that they cannot unmake.
- [ ] Something is **lost, or nearly lost, or gained at a price**.
- [ ] If nothing happened: is the chapter a *deliberate* pressure scene — tension
      without event? If so, is the tension escalating? If not, cut or merge it.
- [ ] Two characters want **incompatible** things in the same room.

> **Fail signal:** the chapter could be summarised as "X talked to Y about Z." That
> summary is a death certificate.

---

## Gate 2 — THE TURN

*Does the chapter end on a turn, and does the prose earn it?*

- [ ] The last five paragraphs contain a turn of one of four kinds:
      **reversal** / **reveal** / **decision** / **arrival**.
- [ ] The turn is **prepared** — a reader who was paying attention could have
      predicted it, which is what makes it satisfying rather than arbitrary.
- [ ] The last three sentences **satisfy** — they sound like something a person
      would say, not like notes.
- [ ] The chapter does **not** end on a summary of feelings.

**Write the last sentence here:**

> _______________________________________________________________

**Does it change anything?** (Yes / No)

---

## Gate 3 — POV DISCIPLINE

- [ ] No head-hopping within a scene.
- [ ] No sentence where the narrator knows more than the POV.
- [ ] The POV's **attention is specific** — there is at least one thing only this
      character would notice.
- [ ] The POV **believes** something, and where they're wrong, the prose is wrong
      with them.
- [ ] The POV holds a **false model** of someone the reader can see clearly.
- [ ] The POV is **wrong** about something.

**Find the narrator-knowledge sentence.** If one exists, it goes.

---

## Gate 4 — DIALOGUE

- [ ] Dialogue is **≥50%** of the scene.
- [ ] Every line does **two jobs**.
- [ ] No line is **interchangeable** (remove the tag — could someone else say it?).
- [ ] Dialogue is in the **right register** for the speaker.
- [ ] Tags: no emotion tags; varied; beats preferred over "said".
- [ ] There is at least one **silence** that means something.
- [ ] The scene opens **mid-conversation** or in motion.

---

## Gate 5 — INTERIORITY

- [ ] Emotion is rendered by **behaviour**, not named.
- [ ] No emotion-noun doing work a gesture could do.
- [ ] No "her heart pounded / eyes widened / breath caught / shiver down spine."
- [ ] Free indirect discourse appears at least once, **marked**.
- [ ] Interiority is **allocated**: does any character get a moment where their
      interiority is the whole point? (If every character's interiority is
      described at the same depth, no character's is.)
- [ ] The ratio is roughly **70/30** action-and-dialogue to interiority.

---

## Gate 6 — CONCRETE

- [ ] Every adjective: is there a physical detail it could be replaced with?
- [ ] Every abstract noun: can it take a number or a material?
- [ ] Every verb: is there a more precise one?
- [ ] No two images in one sentence.
- [ ] No filter verbs (*saw that*, *noticed*, *realised*, *felt*, *seemed*).
- [ ] Adverbs: **one per paragraph maximum**, none at the top of a scene.
- [ ] Numbers are used somewhere.
- [ ] The prose is in the **house register** — no Latinate words unless they're
      marking power.

**Counts:**
- Adverbs: ______ (target: ≤1 per paragraph)
- Filter verbs: ______ (target: ≤2 per chapter)
- "Said": ______ (target: vary with beats)

---

## Gate 7 — WORLD

- [ ] Every block of worldbuilding has **someone who wants it**.
- [ ] The listener **reacts** to information.
- [ ] New proper nouns: ≤4 per chapter.
- [ ] New institutional terms: ≤2 per chapter.
- [ ] New magic rules: ≤1 per chapter, and only if the plot needs it now.
- [ ] Nothing is explained that the reader could discover.
- [ ] No "it was well known that" / "as every scholar knows" / "little did he know."

---

## Gate 8 — VIOLENCE (if present)

- [ ] **Mechanism** is physically accurate.
- [ ] **Consequence** appears.
- [ ] **Aftermath** is in a *different scene*.
- [ ] The prose is short in the act and long in the cost.
- [ ] No glamour shot; the prose does not enjoy it.
- [ ] The wound appears again later, or will.
- [ ] No sexual violence as spectacle; aftermath is rendered and consequences are
      on the page.

---

## Gate 9 — CHARACTER

For the POV of this chapter:

- [ ] **Want** (conscious goal) — specific and named.
- [ ] **Need** — different from the want.
- [ ] **Wound** — a specific event, place, and year.
- [ ] **Lie** — a false belief, in their words.
- [ ] **Fear** — a specific dread.
- [ ] **Contradiction** — two impossible things they hold.
- [ ] Their history **changes something on this page**.
- [ ] They are **good at something** on the page.
- [ ] They are **wrong** about something.

**If the want and the need are the same, stop here and fix the character.**

---

## Gate 10 — ROMANCE MODE (romantasy only)

- [ ] Removing the romance would collapse the book.
- [ ] **Both** parties have full six-part engines.
- [ ] The romance **complicates** the wound; it does not cure it.
- [ ] One party wants it before the other knows they want it.
- [ ] The reader is ahead of the protagonist — but not given the truth.
- [ ] Every joke anchors to something the POV wants.
- [ ] Refusal is **safe** in this book.
- [ ] The world's problems are **personal** to the couple.
- [ ] HEA/HFN is on the table, and the ending will be **expensive**.

---

## Gate 11 — LINE EDIT

- [ ] Read aloud. Note every hesitation. Fix the rhythm.
- [ ] Sentence-length variation: is there a burst-and-turn?
- [ ] Three-beat turn: setup (long) → turn (short) → consequence (long)?
- [ ] Cut every adverb that can go.
- [ ] Cut every filter.
- [ ] Cut one image from any sentence with two.
- [ ] Read the first and last lines of the chapter together. Do they belong to the
      same scene?
- [ ] Read the whole chapter in one breath. Does it flow?

---

## The revision workflow

```
DRAFT        → write it ugly, finish it, don't diagnose
              ↓
GATE 1-3     → story level: stakes, turn, POV
              ↓ (rewrite the scene, not the sentences)
GATE 4-6     → scene level: dialogue, interiority, concreteness
              ↓ (line work)
GATE 7-10    → content level: world, violence, character, romance
              ↓
GATE 11      → read aloud, cut
              ↓
CHAPTER NOTE → write down what the gate caught and what you did about it
```

**The chapter note matters.** At the end of every chapter, three lines:

```
GATE CAUGHT: ______________________________
FIXED BY:    ______________________________
EXCEPTION:   ______________________________ (and why)
```

The exceptions are the most valuable part. They're where the house style learns
something new. Add them to `bible/11-antipatterns.md` if the exception was a
general rule, or to the chapter's own notes if it was local.

## A one-page scene card

*Use before drafting a scene.*

```
SCENE:
POV:                       WANT (in this scene):
WHERE:                     NEED (implied):
WHAT THEY THINK IS TRUE:   OBSTACLE (their belief):
WHAT'S ACTUALLY TRUE:       COST OF SUCCESS:
WHO ELSE IS IN THE ROOM:   WHAT EACH WANTS:
TURN TYPE (rev/rev/dec/arr):
OPENING (in motion / costly / irreversible):
EXIT LINE:
```
