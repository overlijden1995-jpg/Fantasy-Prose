# Research · Method

**How the corpus was assembled, and how the findings were derived.**

---

## 1. The brief

> "Perform an extensive research project on the most popular adult fiction fantasy
> series (low magic, gritty, realistic, think ASOIAF, First Law) and then search for
> popular romantasy series as well. Study the prose and syntax, as well as character
> psychology of the twenty most popular series and use this to create a prose bible
> that will live in this repository."

Translated into research questions:

1. **Which twenty series?** — on the basis of actual popularity, not reputation.
2. **What do they do to the prose?** — diction, syntax, rhythm, POV, dialogue.
3. **What do they do to character psychology?** — how a person is built and rendered.
4. **What transfers?** — which mechanisms are portable into a new book, and which are
   an author's private signature.

---

## 2. Selecting the corpus

### 2.1 The popularity criterion

"Most popular" needed an operational definition. Four independent measures were
used, because any single one is gameable:

| Measure | Source | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit sales** | Publisher and Circana BookScan figures reported in trade press | Commercial mass |
| **Ratings volume** | Goodreads rating counts | Reader base at scale |
| **Goodreads Choice Awards / list placement** | Goodreads | Sustained reader preference across years |
| **Derivative signals** | BookTok/Bookstagram virality, adaptation, long-run reprints | Cultural penetration beyond the core audience |

Sources for the figures are in `03-sources.md`. Where a series has no reliable
sales figure (several have never disclosed), the ratings-volume and list-placement
signals were used instead, and that is noted.

### 2.2 The two pools

**Pool A — Low-magic / gritty / realist** (11 series)
Selected on: popularity + a documented commitment to consequence, moral ambiguity,
and material realism. Includes some series with substantial magic (Malazan, the
Riyria, the Green Bone) because the criterion is *what the magic costs*, not its
absence.

**Pool B — Romantasy** (9 series)
Selected on: the genre's own definition — the romance is load-bearing and the
fantasy world is load-bearing. Includes two series (Love Hypothesis, Outlander) that
are not secondary-world fantasy, because they are the genre's clearest
demonstrations of the *romance-engine* mechanics this house needs, and because they
won the popular imagination.

**Adjacent case study (not counted):** *The Stormlight Archive* — analysed for
comparison and as a counter-example, but excluded from the twenty because its magic
is the plot's engine rather than its cost.

### 2.3 What was excluded and why

| Excluded | Reason |
|---|---|
| *Harry Potter*, *The Lord of the Rings* | Enormously popular, but their prose is documented elsewhere at length and their mechanisms (comic-deck ordering, mythic subordination) are not this house's target. |
| *Mistborn*, *The Stormlight Archive* | Popular, but their magic is the *engine* rather than the cost — a different design brief. Stormlight is retained as an adjacent counter-case. |
| *The Broken Earth*, *The Poppy War*'s siblings | Popular, but adjacent. Kuang's *Babel* is included as a direct comparison because it deliberately inverts the *Poppy War*'s own prose strategy. |
| *Cradle*, *The Wandering Inn* | Very popular; different contract with the reader. |
| Anime-influenced progression fantasy | A large and popular field with its own conventions; out of scope for a realist house. |

## 3. Method of analysis

For each series, four passes:

### Pass 1 — Corpus statistics
What can be counted: series length, book count, POV structure, publication cadence.

### Pass 2 — Published craft analysis
Interviews and craft essays by the authors, where they exist, and academic or
critical analyses where they don't. This is where the *mechanisms* come from, because
the authors themselves are the most reliable witnesses to their own methods.

### Pass 3 — Reception analysis
Reader and reviewer discussion, filtered for *specificity*. Reviews that say
"the prose is beautiful" are noise. Reviews that say "every character sounds
different" or "the info-dump in the first fifty pages killed it" are data — they
tell us what readers actually notice, which is what the bible has to serve.

### Pass 4 — Synthesis into a mechanism
Each finding was rewritten as a **transferable mechanism** with a stated failure
mode. Anything that couldn't be rewritten that way was discarded as authorial
signature rather than craft.

### 3.1 The four questions asked of every series

1. **What does the prose do that the story doesn't need it to do?** (The specific
   contribution of the prose.)
2. **What does the prose refuse to do?**
3. **How is a character built from a wound, a want and a contradiction?**
4. **What is the single most transferable mechanism, and where does it break?**

---

## 4. What was found, at the top level

### 4.1 The five convergences

**1. Prose as an instrument, not a decoration.**
The strongest series in the corpus are *invisible* — one of them explicitly states
its aim as "invisibility," and the other great ones achieve the same effect
without naming it. The prose is judged by whether the reader was thinking about the
story.

**2. POV as a psychological lens, not a camera.**
The most-cited technical achievement across the corpus is that each POV character
*notices different things*, and the narration changes accordingly. This is the
single most transferable technique identified.

**3. Consequence as the source of tension.**
Where the popular fantasy form usually generates tension from plot complexity
(prophecy, destiny, a countdown), the corpus under study generates it from
**costs landing**. A character makes a reasonable decision and is charged for it.

**4. Psychology rendered as behaviour, not as description.**
Across twenty series, the consistent finding is that the writers who work
*describe less*. The emotion is replaced by the evidence for it — a flinch, a
misdirected courtesy, a cup set down too hard.

**5. Violence as a ledger.**
Wounds are tracked across books and change behaviour. Deaths remove people from the
world and the world is smaller afterwards.

### 4.2 The two divergences, and why they're instructive

**Romantasy's prose is hotter, but its *architecture* is the same.** The best
romantasy in the corpus uses exactly the mechanisms of the gritty register —
POV discipline, dialogue doing two jobs, consequence — with a higher sensory
temperature and an interiority budget shifted toward the romantic arc.

**The info-dump is the corpus's dominant failure, in both pools.** The most
frequently criticised prose fault in the studied material is a character
reciting history, genealogies, or world rules to a listener who didn't ask.

## 5. Limitations — stated honestly

1. **Secondary-source bias.** The published craft analysis of these books is
   uneven: Martin, Abercrombie, Le Guin, Gabaldon, and Weeks have given
   substantial craft interviews; others have not. Where the author's own account is
   unavailable, the analysis is a reconstruction from the text and reception.

2. **Reception is not consensus.** Where critical and reader opinion diverges
   sharply (Maas, Kuang, Gwynne), both positions are recorded in the series notes,
   and the bible takes the *constructive* side of each argument — the specific
   criticism, not the dismissal.

3. **Sales figures are partial and lagging.** Several of the most commercially
   significant series (notably *One Dark Window* and the *Empyrean*) have
   publisher-reported figures that lag actual performance. The corpus is described
   as "the twenty most prominent and most discussed," which is more defensible than
   a false precision ranking.

4. **The corpus over-represents English-language prose.** All twenty series are in
   English, which means the study inherits English syntax and cannot speak to
   e.g. how these mechanisms survive translation — which is itself a live question,
   since *A Song of Ice and Fire*'s translation success is partly attributed to its
   plain Germanic vocabulary.

5. **This is a synthesis, not a survey.** The findings are the ones that
   transferred into a coherent house style. Findings that didn't transfer are
   noted in the series notes as **not adopted**, with the reason — which is itself
   useful, since it maps the boundaries of the house.

---

## 6. How to extend this

The bible is a **v1.0** and the research is ongoing. To extend it:

1. **Add a series** to `01-corpus.md` with its figures and sources, then run the
   four passes in `02-series-notes.md`.
2. **Add a mechanism** to the relevant `bible/` chapter, with its failure mode.
3. **Record exceptions** in `bible/12-chapter-diagnostic.md` chapter notes; when an
   exception recurs, promote it to a rule.
4. **Re-run the diagnostic** on every chapter, and note what it keeps catching.
   The rules that keep catching real problems are the ones worth keeping.
