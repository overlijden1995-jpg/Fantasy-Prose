# Fantasy-Prose

**A prose bible for adult fantasy: low-magic, gritty, realist — and its romantasy counterpart.**

This repository is not a manuscript. It is a *toolkit*: a codified description of how
prose is built at the sentence, scene, and psychology level, derived from an extensive
study of **twenty popular adult fantasy series** — eleven in the low-magic / gritty /
realist register (*A Song of Ice and Fire*, *The First Law*, *Realm of the Elderlings*,
*The Gentlemen Bastards*, *The Green Bone Saga*, *The Second Apocalypse*, *The
Bloodsworn Saga*, *Night Angel*, *The Riyria Revelations*, *The Poppy War*,
*Malazan*) and nine in the romantasy register (*A Court of Thorns and Roses*, *The
Empyrean*, *Throne of Glass*, *Crescent City*, *One Dark Window*, *The Queen of the
Tearling*, *Outlander*, *The Love Hypothesis*, *The Serpent and the Wings of Night*)
— plus one adjacent counter-case (*The Stormlight Archive*).

Everything here is written to be **used**, not admired. Every principle has a
diagnostic. Every principle has original sample prose demonstrating it. Nothing is
here that cannot be applied to a chapter tomorrow.

## The one-paragraph version

We write **adult fantasy that behaves like it really happened**. Low magic, high
consequence. The prose is plain-spoken, Anglo-Saxon-leaning, unhurried at the level of
the word and relentless at the level of the thought. Character is carried by *what
people notice* and *what they cannot stop doing*, not by adjectives. Violence has a
aftermath that lasts longer than the scene. Magic — where it exists — is a resource
with a price, a body, and a bill that comes due. The reader is never told what to
feel; the reader is given enough accurate detail to arrive at a feeling and be
surprised by it.

We are not writing Tolkien. We are not writing *A Court of Thorns and Roses*. We are
writing the thing that both of those are reaching for, without their weaknesses.

## Working agreements

1. **No pastiche.** We do not imitate a living author's surface. We study *mechanisms*
   — how a sentence is built, how a chapter turns, how a mind is rendered — and build
   our own register from them.
2. **Every rule has a counter-rule.** Any rule strong enough to be useful is also
   strong enough to be misused. Each chapter names its failure mode.
3. **Original examples only.** All sample prose in `samples/` was written for this
   repository. Nothing is quoted from the studied works.
4. **The bible is a compass, not a cage.** Where a law and a specific story conflict,
   the story wins. Record the exception in the diagnostic so we learn from it.
